byggsteg is a CI/CD orchestrator written in Lisp (Guile Scheme), leverages SXML, SQLite3, some POSIX / UNIX utilities, and the mighty GNU Artanis web-framework.

If you like my work, please support me by [[https://bmc.link/jjbigorra][buying me a cup of coffee ☕]] so I can continue with a lot of motivation.

* What is byggsteg ?

byggsteg is a free software project with the aim of developing a suite of functionalities to allow engineers to deliver software quicker and with less friction.

View my live instance of byggsteg here: https://byggsteg.jointhefreeworld.org

The aim of byggsteg is to release you from your dependency on proprietary systems like GitHub actions.
This allows you to create continuous integration and continuous delivery (CI & CD) pipelines in an easy way that are tailored to your needs and which you fully control.

Easily deploy stuff, for example to your private VPS, self-hosting or to cloud.
/byggsteg/ means "build step" in the Norwegian language.

I don't mind having the software building and testing happening in the same machine as where I run the production workloads, since I am looking for supreme cost-efficiency, but it's highly recommended that you do separate these things, if you can afford it.

* Jobs and profiles

Jobs are simple Lisp (Guile Scheme) code. Profiles are just saved jobs.

byggsteg allows you to send code over the wire (HTTP) and save it too, in a thunk form, for example :
#+begin_src  scheme
  (lambda()
    `((project . "kracht")
      (branch-name . "trunk")
      (task . "maak-test")
      (clone-url . "https://codeberg.org/jjba23/kracht")))
#+end_src

Sending things in thunk form allows one to execute any code arbitrarily, inside the lambda expression. Be responsible and wary of who you give access to your byggsteg instance.

#+begin_src scheme
(lambda()
  (define my-project-name "wikimusic-api")
  (define my-branch-name
    (format #f "branch-num-~a" (* 2 (+ 3 (+ 8 10)))))

  ;; remember that a ". ," and a "unquote" are equivalent
  `((project . ,my-project-name)
    (branch-name unquote my-branch-name)
    (task . ("maak-test" "maak-build"))
    (clone-url unquote (format #f "https://codeberg.org/jjba23/~a" my-project-name))))
#+end_src

* Pipelines, artifacts, and watching a job run

A thunk may answer with a *list* of jobs instead of one, in which case they run in order, as a pipeline, and the first one that fails stops those behind it. The stages share one row and one log:

#+begin_src scheme
(lambda()
  (list `((project . "wikimusic-api")
          (branch-name . "trunk")
          (task . "maak-test")
          (clone-url . "https://codeberg.org/jjba23/wikimusic-api"))
        `((project . "wikimusic-api")
          (branch-name . "trunk")
          (task . "maak-build")
          ;; what to keep, relative to the clone: a bare path, or a (name . paths) bundle
          (artifacts . ("dist/wikimusic-api"
                        ("docs" . ("docs/manual"))))
          (clone-url . "https://codeberg.org/jjba23/wikimusic-api"))))
#+end_src

What a job prints reaches its log /while it runs/, in batches, so there is something to watch rather than a console that fills up at the end. What it collects is packed into gzipped tarballs once its commands are over -- whether they passed or failed, since a build that broke halfway is exactly when its partial output is worth keeping -- and is downloadable from its log page. The bare paths of an ~artifacts~ field share one tarball, offered under the job's title; each ~(name . paths)~ bundle is packed into a tarball of its own and offered under the name it was given, so one stage can keep several things apart.

A job that failed is offered a retry in the list of logs, and any job that is not still running is offered one on its own page. The deployment as a whole can be read at ~/activity~ (a year of job runs, drawn like a commit heatmap) and searched at ~/search~ across jobs, logs and profiles.

All of it is described at length in the [[file:docs/manual/en.org][manual]].

* On Reproducibility: Why GNU Guix?

A build should never fail because "it worked on my machine." Reliable software development requires explicit, bit-for-bit reproducibility and a functional approach to package management and system deployment.

#+BEGIN_EXPORT html
<a href="https://guix.gnu.org/">
  <img src="https://jointhefreeworld.org/ggg/dist/gnu-guix.svg" alt="GNU Guix logo" style="max-width: 250px; width: 100%; height: auto;" />
</a>
#+END_EXPORT

- *Declarative Build Graphs:* Packages are pure functions of their inputs with no implicit system state.
- *Programmable with Guile:* Full system and package configurations written in [[https://www.gnu.org/software/guile/][Guile Scheme]] rather than rigid DSLs or static files.
- *Time Travel:* Rebuild the exact system state from any point in git history using ~guix time-machine~.
- *Atomic Operations:* Instantly upgrade or roll back environments without leaving orphaned dependencies or broken states.
- *Unified Tooling:* One tool manages development shells, isolated containers, package builds, and full system configurations.
